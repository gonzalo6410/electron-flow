Electron Flow

editing

Click an atom to select it. Press a letter to add an atom bonded to it: C N O F S P B H, and L for Cl, R for Br, I for iodine, A for Na, K, M for Mg. Or click an element in the table, or drag one onto an atom. Delete removes the selected atom with its hydrogens. Shift-click another atom to bond or unbond the two. Plus and minus set the overall charge. Shift-H fills every atom's usual bonds with hydrogens. The engine works out bond orders, lone pairs and charges from what is there; to make a double bond, take hydrogens away.

moves

Drag from a pair of dots or from a bond to an atom or to a bond to draw an arrow for one pair of electrons. A lone pair can only become a bond to its own atom; a bond's pair can become a lone pair on one of its atoms or a bond from one of them to a third atom. Enter, or the button, makes the move: if no bond is made or broken the arrows show resonance and the picture changes to that structure; if a bond is made or broken the move plays from before to after, every bond and charge sliding to its new value. A move that would give an atom more than its octet, or leave a hydrogen with nothing, is refused with the reason. Pick a reaction to get the reagents placed together; the button shows the textbook arrows. To bring in a second compound, search for it and press add beside its name (or shift-enter).

names

Type a common name, a systematic name, an abbreviation (THF, DMSO, LDA) or a SMILES. The names and synonyms of about 60,000 compounds come from PubChem and are part of the page; a compound the engine cannot draw yet is listed with the reason. A systematic name is understood only if it is in that list; anything else can be typed as a SMILES. A bare ion is shown with a counter-ion beside it, a sodium for a negative charge and a chloride for a positive one.

rules

The octet is never exceeded. Contributing structures are the ones with the fewest octet holes. A sigma bond breaks only where the octet forces it. In a small ring two neighbouring atoms may share one extra bond in the ring's plane, as in benzyne; electrons in the ring's plane stay where they are from one structure to the next. No formal charge beyond plus or minus one unless every structure has one. All contributing structures weigh the same. Structures that are the same picture with atoms renamed form one family and alternate on their own; different structures are stepped through with the arrows. Shapes follow the electron groups around each atom; bond lengths and angles are relaxed as springs.
